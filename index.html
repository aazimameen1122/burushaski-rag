How it works

One small engine, implemented twice with identical behaviour — in Python (standard library only) and in JavaScript for this page. No embeddings service, no vector database, no LLM.

1

Chunk & index

Documents are split into heading-aware chunks (~900 characters, 150 overlap). Dictionary entries are indexed one per chunk so a word lookup lands on exactly one entry.

2

Retrieve

A hybrid score — 0.75 × BM25 + 6.0 × TF-IDF cosine — ranks chunks for your question. The best match is shown first, with its source file, section and score.

3

Answer, or refuse

Answers are extractive: text taken from the retrieved chunks, never generated freely. If 40% or more of a question's terms appear nowhere in the corpus, the system refuses rather than hallucinate.

python3 ask.py "What is the Burushaski word for house?"
# -> ha (noun) · ha · /ha/ · "A-ha Hari Parbat-ulo bila." — My house is in Hari Parbat.

Limitations

Retrieval is lexical, not neural: a question phrased with synonyms the corpus never uses can miss. Answers are extractive and can read plainly; a language model behind the same retrieve-and-cite contract is the planned next step. The corpus is the archive as it stands — 62 verified entries — and grows as the archive does.
